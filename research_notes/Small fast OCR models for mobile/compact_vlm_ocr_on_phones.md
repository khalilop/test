## 1. Model inventory: release date, developer, size, quantized footprint, licence, languages, outputs

### Takeaway
By November 2026 the field has converged on 0.8–1.3B document parsers (OvisOCR2, Xiaomi-OCR-0, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, GLM-OCR, MiniCPM-OCR 1.0, OXR-1.0, TeleOCR, MinerU2.5-Pro) that all score 95–97 on OmniDocBench v1.6; the genuinely tiny options are OpenDoc-0.1B (0.1B), Granite-Docling-258M / SmolDocling-256M, Dolphin-1.5 (0.3B) and 256–500M general VLMs (SmolVLM/SmolVLM2, LFM2.5-VL-450M, Moondream 0.5B). Quantized footprints are roughly 0.3–0.4 GB for the 250–500M class and 1.2–1.6 GB for the 0.8–1.3B class, because the vision tower/projector is normally kept at fp16 and often outweighs the 4-bit language model.

### Cited Findings

#### A. OCR / document specialists below 1B parameters
- **OpenDoc-0.1B** (OpenOCR project, released 2025-12-25): two-stage system, PP-DocLayoutV2 layout model + rebuilt UniRec-0.1B recogniser (text, formulas, tables); "only 0.1 billion parameters"; Chinese + English only; outputs Markdown / JSON / visualisation; CPU inference supported via `--gpus -1`; repo is Apache-2.0; UniRec-0.1B itself released 2025-08-20. No file sizes, speeds, ONNX/mobile notes or per-model licence on the page. [F] — [OpenOCR README](https://github.com/topdu/OpenOCR); [opendoc.md](https://github.com/Topdu/OpenOCR/blob/main/docs/opendoc.md)
- **SmolDocling-256M-preview** (IBM Research + Hugging Face, March 2025): licence listed as CDLA-Permissive-2.0; model card now points users to Granite-Docling as the supported successor. [S] — [SmolDocling HF card](https://huggingface.co/docling-project/SmolDocling-256M-preview); [arXiv 2503.11576](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11576v1)
- **Granite-Docling-258M** (IBM, announced 2025-09-17; HF listing updated 2025-09-23): ~258M parameters, Apache 2.0; replaces SmolLM2 with a Granite 3-based LM and SigLIP with SigLIP2; fixes SmolDocling's tendency to get stuck repeating a token; IBM says larger ~512M and ~900M variants are planned and all future Granite-Docling models will stay below 1B. [S] — [IBM announcement](https://ibm.com/new/announcements/granite-docling-end-to-end-document-conversion); [InfoQ, Oct 2025](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/10/granite-docling-ibm)
- Granite-Docling quantized sizes: a community GGUF repo lists Q4_K_M ≈133 MB, Q5_K_M ≈139 MB, Q6_K ≈164 MB, Q8_0 ≈170 MB, F16 ≈317 MB for the LM plus a separate 182 MB F16 vision projector (mmproj) that is required (so ≈315 MB at Q4). Exact repo among the several community conversions was not confirmed. [S] — [Mungert GGUF](https://huggingface.co/Mungert/granite-docling-258M-GGUF); [SandLogic GGUF](https://huggingface.co/SandLogicTechnologies/granite-docling-258M-GGUF)

[F] — [edge-compat card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/granite-docling-258m/CARD.md); [S] — [litert-community card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/granite-docling-258M)

- **Dolphin-1.5** (ByteDance, 2025-10-16): 0.3B, MIT; analyse-then-parse two-stage design; outputs structured JSON + Markdown, element-level text/table/formula/code; deployment via Transformers, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM (no CPU/ONNX/Core ML mention). Dolphin v1 = 2025-05-20 (0.3B); Dolphin-v2 = 2025-12-12, 3B. [F] — [Dolphin README](https://github.com/bytedance/Dolphin)
- A community Core ML conversion "Dolphin on Apple Neural Engine" exists (pkhairkh/bytedance-dolphin1.5-coreml); no details retrieved. [S] — [HF mirror listing](https://huggingface-proxy-6ci.pages.dev/pkhairkh/bytedance-dolphin1.5-coreml)

[S] — [GOT-OCR-2.0-hf card](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/GOT-OCR-2.0-hf); [HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/GOT-OCR2_0/discussions/35); [community GGUF](https://huggingface.co/cstr/got-ocr2-crispembed-GGUF)

[S] — [community GGUF](https://huggingface.co/SanjeevSOLANKI/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6-GGUF); [official GGUF](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6-GGUF); [F] — [edge-compat card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/paddleocr-vl-1.6/CARD.md)

[F] — [GLM-OCR README](https://github.com/zai-org/GLM-OCR); [S] — [Gigazine 2026-02-04](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260204-z-ai-glm-ocr)
- **MonkeyOCRv2** (July 2026, arXiv 2607.11562): 0.7B parser plus a small vision backbone, 17 languages, Apache-2.0; earlier MonkeyOCR 3B (2025-06-05, zh/en, weights non-commercial) and MonkeyOCR-pro-1.2B (2025-07-10). [F] — [MonkeyOCR README](https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/MonkeyOCR); [S] — [arXiv listing](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2607.11562)

[S] — [OvisOCR2 HF](https://huggingface.co/ATH-MaaS/OvisOCR2); [paper summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2607.13639); [F] — [edge-compat card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/ovisocr2-int8/CARD.md)

### Inferences
- "Smallest that is still a real document OCR model": OpenDoc-0.1B is the smallest with a leaderboard score (but zh/en only, needs a separate layout model, no phone port found); Granite-Docling-258M is the smallest with a measured phone run and a permissive licence; Dolphin-1.5 (0.3B, MIT) sits between them.
- Parameter count understates phone footprint: for PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 the 4-bit LM is 286 MB but the BF16 projector is ~0.85 GB, and for MiniCPM-OCR the mmproj (0.9 GB) is larger than the LM (0.5 GB). Vision-tower quantization (not LM quantization) is what decides whether a 0.9B model fits a low-RAM phone.
- Licence filter for a commercial mobile app: clean Apache/MIT = Granite-Docling, PaddleOCR-VL family, GLM-OCR, LightOnOCR, OvisOCR2, Xiaomi-OCR-0, MiniCPM-OCR/MiniCPM-V, MonkeyOCRv2, Dolphin, DeepSeek-OCR-2, SmolVLM, Qwen3.5, Gemma 4. Restricted or unclear = FastVLM (research only), MonkeyOCR v1 weights, Chandra, HunyuanOCR, LFM (revenue cap), OXR, MinerU, Nanonets, GOT-OCR 2.0, dots.ocr (MIT + extra terms).
- Most top sub-1.3B parsers are two-stage (layout detector + crop recogniser: PaddleOCR-VL, GLM-OCR, MiniCPM-OCR, OpenDoc, OXR, MonkeyOCR); a phone app must ship and run both models, while OvisOCR2, Xiaomi-OCR-0, LightOnOCR, Granite-Docling, DeepSeek-OCR and dots.ocr are single-pass.

- Not verified in this session (search budget exhausted; background knowledge only, needs a source before use): Florence-2 base/large parameter counts (~0.23B/0.77B), June 2024 release and MIT licence; SmolVLM/SmolVLM2 exact release months and licences; Granite-Docling language coverage; DeepSeek-OCR v1 release date, licence and languages; HunyuanOCR language count and territorial licence limits; Phi-3.5-vision / Phi-4-multimodal (both above 3B) and H2OVL-Mississippi release/licence details; Moondream licence; Gemma 3 4B details.

- No Core ML, MLX-Swift or LiteRT sizes were found for GLM-OCR, LightOnOCR-2, MonkeyOCRv2, TeleOCR, Xiaomi-OCR-0, OpenDoc-0.1B or Dolphin-1.5.

### Inferences
- The three OCR-specialist phone builds (Granite-Docling, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, OvisOCR2) behave opposite ways: Granite-Docling runs only on CPU (GPU fails), while the two 0.8–0.9B models ran on flagship GPUs but failed on CPU on both Android test phones (OvisOCR2 does run on the iPhone 17 Pro CPU). As of September 2026 no LiteRT build of a 95+-score OCR model had been shown working on the mid-range Pixel 8a (CPU runs failed; its GPU was not tested for these two models and failed to load the sibling Qwen3.5-0.8B-VL build).
- The S26 GPU decode rate for PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (35.6 tok/s) is consistent with the unattributed "22 s page / 17 s table" timings being flagship-GPU results (≈780 and ≈600 output tokens).
- GPU paths cost memory: ~3.8–4.0 GB peak on GPU versus 0.6–0.9 GB on CPU for the same 0.8B model on iPhone 17 Pro, and 3.8 GB versus 1.6 GB on Android. On 6–8 GB phones the CPU path is the one that fits.
- Sub-500M general VLMs are 2–3× faster than 0.8–0.9B OCR models on the same phone (72–103 tok/s vs 35 tok/s on Galaxy S26).

- No measured phone numbers (tokens/s, seconds/page, RAM) were found for MiniCPM-OCR 1.0, GLM-OCR, Xiaomi-OCR-0, LightOnOCR-2, MonkeyOCRv2, TeleOCR, OpenDoc-0.1B, Dolphin-1.5, GOT-OCR 2.0, Florence-2 (speed), FastVLM (absolute TTFT on a named iPhone), Moondream 0.5B or SmolVLM-256M.
- Peak-RAM figures are missing for most Android runs; no energy (mAh/J per page) measurement was found for any OCR VLM.
- All edge-compat numbers come from one author's devices and short prompts (≈200–300 prompt tokens); they are not independent replications and most are not full-page OCR runs.

## 3. Mobile runtimes for multimodal models as of late 2026, and their limits

### Takeaway
llama.cpp (mtmd) and Google's LiteRT-LM are the two runtimes with real OCR-specialist model support in 2026: llama.cpp merged PaddleOCR-VL, GLM-OCR, DeepSeek-OCR, HunyuanOCR and dots.ocr between February and April 2026, and LiteRT-LM has community conversions of PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, OvisOCR2 and Granite-Docling. MNN, ExecuTorch, MLC, Core ML / Core AI, GenieX and the app layer (PocketPal, AI Edge Gallery, MiniCPM apps) mainly cover general VLMs, and GPU/NPU acceleration is in practice limited to recent Snapdragon (Adreno 700+/Hexagon) and Apple devices.

### Cited Findings
- **llama.cpp (libmtmd)**: officially lists pre-quantized vision GGUFs for Gemma 3 (4B+), SmolVLM/SmolVLM2, Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL 2.5/3, Moondream2 and Gemma 4 E2B/E4B (default Q4_K_M); notes that "OCR models are trained with specific prompt and input structure" and links support PRs for PaddleOCR-VL, GLM-OCR, DeepSeek-OCR, dots.ocr and HunyuanOCR; mtmd README adds MiniCPM-V 4.6 and legacy MiniCPM-V/o, GLM-Edge, MobileVLM. [F] — [multimodal.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md); [mtmd README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/tree/master/tools/mtmd)

## 5. Failure modes that matter on device

### Takeaway
The documented failures are silent rewriting of imperfect text, runaway repetition on sparse tables/forms or over-dense pages, quantization that corrupts structured output, and fixed low-resolution vision inputs in phone conversions that cause the model to "invent and loop" on small print. Sustained generation also throttles: one iPhone test kept only 38–67% of burst speed after ten minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Silent correction: "Do VLMs Read or Rewrite?" (arXiv 2607.21617, 2026) finds that when text has typos or unclear characters VLMs often output what they expect, e.g. "prbolem" → "problem", across English, Chinese and Korean. A 2026 study on Ancient Greek editions reports errors that stay fluent and plausible. [S] — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21617); [paper summary](https://chatpaper.com/de/paper/287387)
- Repetition loops: OCR VLMs with smaller language backbones "often enter infinite repetition loops when parsing sparse tables and forms"; streaming detection plus masking/retry cut worst-case latency from 60 s+ to ~10–20 s (vendor blog, Feb 2026). An Arabic manuscript OCR study reports a repetition-loop failure under greedy decoding. Granite-Docling was built partly to fix SmolDocling's token-repetition instability. [S] — [Tensorlake blog](https://tensorlake.ai/blog/vlm-pipeline-improvement); [arXiv 2608.22366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22366); [IBM announcement](https://ibm.com/new/announcements/granite-docling-end-to-end-document-conversion)
- Resolution limit on phone builds: for OvisOCR2 at a static 512×512 input, pages with 9 pt text exceed what the encoder can resolve — "the model transcribes the start correctly, then invents and loops"; tiling is suggested. Granite-Docling at single 512×512: dense multi-column pages and small formulas degrade; using the runtime's internal resampler instead of bilinear pre-resize can make the model hallucinate a page; dropping one newline in the chat format yields a blank hallucinated page. [F] — [OvisOCR2 card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/ovisocr2-int8/CARD.md); [Granite-Docling card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/granite-docling-258m/CARD.md)
- Quantization damage: for PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 int4 and integer-compute int8 "measurably corrupt transcription" (so the decoder ships in fp16, 1.33 GB); for Granite-Docling integer-compute int8/int4 corrupts DocTags structure and int4 was rejected; LightOnOCR GGUF repos advise against quantizing the vision encoder. [F] — [PaddleOCR-VL card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/paddleocr-vl-1.6/CARD.md); [Granite-Docling card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/granite-docling-258m/CARD.md); [S] — [LightOnOCR GGUF](https://huggingface.co/SandLogicTechnologies/LightOnOCR-2-1B-GGUF)
- Backend-dependent quality: Qwen3.5-0.8B-VL scored 8/8 on a test set on Mac, 6/8 on iPhone 17 Pro Metal and 3/8 on iPhone CPU (reproducible, cause not established); expect "fluent paraphrases rather than token-exact matches, with weaknesses in counting and dense layouts". PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 produced a repeated-character flood on a text-only prompt on CPU. SmolVLM2-500M: pure greedy decoding can become repetitive or verbose. [F] — [Qwen3.5 card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/qwen35-0.8b-vl-int8/CARD.md); [PaddleOCR-VL card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/paddleocr-vl-1.6/CARD.md); [SmolVLM2 card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/smolvlm2-500m/CARD.md)
- Prompt fragility: OCR specialists are task-prompted, not chat models — PaddleOCR-VL gives no output for anything other than a built-in task prompt and loses accuracy with multi-turn history; OvisOCR2 on iPhone echoed the prompt on text questions; tiny LFM2.5-VL-450M "can miss" fine-grained visual questions and is recommended for coarse tasks and large-text OCR. [F] — [ai-client PR](https://github.com/matsudamper/ai-client/pull/250); [OvisOCR2 card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/ovisocr2-int8/CARD.md); [LFM2.5-VL card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/lfm25-vl-450m-int4/CARD.md)
- Bounding boxes: available from dots.ocr (layout JSON boxes), Chandra (chunk boxes), PaddleOCR-VL-1.5+ (text-line spotting, polygonal localisation), LightOnOCR-2 (separate bbox checkpoint), LFM2.5-VL-450M (box prediction) and OXR (layout coordinates); the default LightOnOCR model is OCR-only and two-stage systems get block-level boxes from the layout model, not the VLM. [F] — [dots.ocr](https://github.com/rednote-hilab/dots.ocr); [Chandra](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra); [PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 doc](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/docs/version3.x/algorithm/PaddleOCR-VL/PaddleOCR-VL-1.5.en.md); [OXR](https://github.com/SoMarkAI/OXR); [S] — [LightOnOCR-2 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/lightonai/lightonocr-2)

[S] — [Gemma 3n card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3n-E4B-it-litert-preview); [F] — edge-compat cards above; [DeepSeek-OCR-2](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR-2)

[F] — [CoreML-LLM README](https://github.com/john-rocky/CoreML-LLM); [S] — [MiniCPM-V-4.6-CoreAI](https://huggingface.co/coreai-community/MiniCPM-V-4.6-CoreAI)

[S] — [arXiv 2507.08505](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08505v2)

[S] — [Apple developer forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834805)
- Hallucination is now benchmarked: Tencent released CHAOS-Bench, a character-level hallucination benchmark, on 2026-07-13; MiniCPM-OCR ships a separate 2B "Jev" verifier that scores OCR output correctness (AUROC 93.6). [F] — [HunyuanOCR README](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanOCR); [MiniCPM-OCR README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR)

### Inferences
- A VLM error looks like valid text, whereas a classic recogniser's error usually looks like garbage with a low confidence score; none of the small VLMs exposes per-word confidence, so silent rewriting is the hardest failure to detect on device (a second verifier model such as Jev costs another 2B parameters).
- The benchmark scores in section 4 assume native or high-resolution input; a phone build pinned to 512×512 or 560×560 cannot read a full A4 page of small print in one pass. Real deployments must tile or crop (layout model + per-region calls), which multiplies vision-encoder passes and time.
- Because output length drives latency and loops inflate it without bound, a hard `max_tokens` cap plus repetition detection is mandatory on a phone.

### Gaps
- No measurement of battery drain (energy per page) for any OCR VLM on a phone was found; the OnePlus 13R paper's temperature and power numbers were not retrievable.
- No study was found that quantifies hallucination or silent-correction rates specifically for sub-1B OCR models, or after int4/int8 quantization.

## 6. Realistic time for one receipt or one dense page, by phone tier

### Takeaway
Measured: a flagship needs roughly 20–35 s for a dense page with a sub-1B OCR VLM (35.4 s for a 1,025-token page with Granite-Docling on a Galaxy S26 CPU; 22 s reported for PaddleOCR-VL-1.6), and seconds for a short receipt. For mid-range phones only decode rates exist (16–23 tok/s on a Pixel 8a CPU), and for 3–4 GB phones no measurement exists at all.

### Cited Findings
- Flagship, measured page: Granite-Docling-258M 35.4 s/page for 1,025 output tokens (Galaxy S26, CPU, LiteRT-LM 0.16.1). [S] — [litert-community card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/granite-docling-258M)
- Flagship, reported page: PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 22 s page OCR and 17 s table recognition on a synthetic report page (device not stated; Galaxy S26 GPU decode rate is 35.6 tok/s with 0.19 s TTFT). [F] — [edge-compat card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/paddleocr-vl-1.6/CARD.md)
- Flagship decode rates for OCR-capable models: 33.5–35.6 tok/s (Granite-Docling CPU, OvisOCR2 GPU, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 GPU on Galaxy S26); 48.5 tok/s (OvisOCR2, iPhone 17 Pro GPU); 18.1 tok/s (OvisOCR2, iPhone 17 Pro CPU). [F] — edge-compat cards cited in section 2
- Flagship, short structured outputs: LFM2.5-VL-450M 2.4 s per 512×512 image on Galaxy S25 Ultra; SmolVLM 500M ≈7 s per image (app developer figure). [S] — [Liquid blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-vl-450m); [Off Grid benchmarks](https://www.mintlify.com/alichherawalla/off-grid-mobile-ai/technical/performance-benchmarks)
- Mid-range evidence: Pixel 8a (Tensor G3) CPU decode 23.3 tok/s (Granite-Docling, TTFT 2.34 s), 15.8–19.9 tok/s (Qwen3.5-0.8B-VL, TTFT 1.3–1.5 s, 1.57 GB peak), 22.6 tok/s CPU / 33.1 tok/s GPU (LFM2.5-VL-450M); PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 and OvisOCR2 failed to run on its CPU. App-developer figure for 6–8 GB phones: SmolVLM 500M ≈15 s (or 20–30 s) per image, Qwen3-VL 2B ≈35 s. [F] — edge-compat cards in section 2; [S] — [Off Grid benchmarks](https://www.mintlify.com/alichherawalla/off-grid-mobile-ai/technical/performance-benchmarks)
- Low-end proxy (not a phone): Raspberry Pi 5 CPU runs Qwen3.5-0.8B-VL at 6.9 tok/s decode, 71.6 tok/s prefill, 3.8 s TTFT, 2,117 MB peak. [F] — [Qwen3.5 card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/qwen35-0.8b-vl-int8/CARD.md)
- Vendor RAM floors: MiniCPM-OCR 1.0 ≥6 GB; MiniCPM-V 4.7 1B ≥4 GB; Off Grid vision ≥6 GB; MNN Chat warns budget devices may fail. [F] — [MiniCPM-V-Apps](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-V-Apps); [MNN Chat README](https://github.com/alibaba/MNN/blob/master/apps/Android/MnnLlmChat/README.md)
- Classic baseline timing: PP-OCRv5 detector 8.6–17.6 ms and recogniser 5.9–8.0 ms per crop on Galaxy S26 NPU/GPU; 460 ms and 86 ms per crop on Raspberry Pi 5 CPU with 135–206 MB peak memory. [F] — [det card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/pp-ocrv5__ppocr_det_fp16/CARD.md); [rec card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/pp-ocrv5__ppocr_rec_fp16/CARD.md)

### Inferences
- Everything in this list is an estimate computed from the measured decode rates above, not a measurement. Assumed output sizes: receipt ≈150–400 tokens; dense A4 page ≈800–1,500 tokens (the measured Granite-Docling page was 1,025 tokens; DocTags/Markdown/HTML tables inflate counts).
- (a) 2024–2026 flagship, 0.8–0.9B OCR model at 35–50 tok/s: receipt ≈3–11 s; dense page ≈20–45 s, plus 0.2–2 s for image encoding/prefill. Sustained multi-page jobs will slow by a third or more from thermal throttling (section 5).
- (b) Mid-range (Pixel 8a-class CPU, 16–23 tok/s): receipt ≈7–25 s; dense page ≈45–90 s — if a build runs at all; the LiteRT builds of PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 and OvisOCR2 failed on the Pixel 8a CPU as of September 2026 (GPU untested), leaving Granite-Docling-258M, Qwen3.5-0.8B-VL or llama.cpp GGUF builds (unmeasured).
- (c) Old phone with 3–4 GB RAM: no numbers exist. Using the Raspberry Pi 5 as an optimistic stand-in (6.9 tok/s for a 0.8B model): receipt ≈25–60 s, dense page ≈2–3.5 minutes. In practice 0.8–1.3B models (1.2–1.6 GB files, 1.5–2.1 GB CPU peak) are unlikely to stay alive in a 3–4 GB phone's foreground memory budget; only ≤500M models (SmolVLM2-500M 0.47–0.88 GB peak, Granite-Docling 322 MB file, Moondream 0.5B 0.8 GB) plausibly fit, at unknown speed.
- Same receipt with PP-OCRv5 (one detection + ~40 line crops): ≈0.25–0.35 s on a flagship NPU/GPU and ≈4 s on the Raspberry Pi 5-class CPU — one to two orders of magnitude faster at under a tenth of the memory.

### Gaps
- No end-to-end seconds-per-page or seconds-per-receipt measurement on a named mid-range or old phone was found for any OCR-specialist VLM; the mid-range and old-phone figures above are arithmetic from decode rates.
- Two-stage pipelines (layout + per-region VLM calls, e.g. MiniCPM-OCR on phones) add layout time and repeated vision encoding; no on-phone timing breakdown is published.

## 7. When a small VLM is worth it over a classic detector + recogniser on mobile

### Takeaway
The benchmark evidence supports a VLM for structure — tables, formulas, reading order, whole-page Markdown, and task-prompted extraction — where 0.8–0.9B models beat the classic pipeline by 12–14 points; it does not support one for plain text lines, word-level boxes, low latency, or low-RAM phones, where a 26 MB PP-OCR-class pipeline is faster by one to two orders of magnitude and on vendor tests more accurate than even very large VLMs.

### Cited Findings
- Structure gap on OmniDocBench v1.6: formula CDM 97.5 (PaddleOCR-VL-1.6) and 97.5 (OvisOCR2) versus 83.1 (MinerU pipeline) and 85.2 (Marker); table TEDS 94.8 / 94.6 versus 81.9 / 65.8; text edit distance 0.033 / 0.027 versus 0.055 / 0.157. [F] — [OmniDocBench README](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)
- Text spotting gap in the other direction (vendor in-house test): PP-OCRv6_medium (34.5M) 86.2 detection Hmean / 83.2 recognition versus 38.3–46.8 / 64.2–74.9 for Qwen3-VL-235B, GPT-5.5 and Gemini-3.1-Pro; even PP-OCRv6_tiny (1.5M) reaches 80.6 / 73.5. [F] — [PP-OCRv6 doc](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/docs/version3.x/algorithm/PP-OCRv6/PP-OCRv6.en.md)
- Cost gap on the same phone (Galaxy S26): PP-OCRv5 9.6 MB + 16.4 MB files, 6–18 ms per stage; PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 1.33 GB, 35.6 tok/s, ~22 s per page. [F] — [det card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/pp-ocrv5__ppocr_det_fp16/CARD.md); [PaddleOCR-VL card](https://github.com/john-rocky/edge-compat/blob/main/cards/paddleocr-vl-1.6/CARD.md)
- Capabilities only VLMs offer in one model: tables to HTML/OTSL, formulas to LaTeX, charts, seals, cross-page table merging, key-information extraction, 109–111 languages in one 0.9B model. [F] — [PaddleOCR README](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR); [MiniCPM-OCR README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR); [S] — [Xiaomi-OCR-0 coverage](https://alphasignal.ai/news/xiaomi-releases-xiaomi-ocr-0-a-tiny-0-8b-model-that-reads-documents-like-bigger)
- Structured extraction is still weak at this size: Xiaomi-OCR-0 scores 59.09% on its in-house key-information-extraction benchmark; with PaddleOCR-VL in llama.cpp, requesting structured output made results too short. [S] — [arXiv 2609.36136](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36136); [F] — [llama.cpp PR #18825](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/18825)
- Handwriting: Chandra (≈5B) lists handwriting and forms; MonkeyOCR v1 states handwriting and photographed text are not fully supported; Gemma 4's card lists handwriting recognition; OXR shows Chinese/English handwriting examples. [F] — [Chandra](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra); [MonkeyOCR](https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/MonkeyOCR); [OXR](https://github.com/SoMarkAI/OXR); [S] — [Gemma 4 coverage](https://edge-ai-vision.com/2026/04/google-pushes-multimodal-ai-further-onto-edge-devices-with-gemma-4)
- Hybrid is what vendors ship: Google's Prompt API guidance pairs a traditional text-extraction model with Gemini Nano for classification; MinerU keeps a CPU "basic" ONNX pipeline (2 GB RAM) beside its VLM tiers; the top parsers call a layout detector first. [S] — [ML Kit GenAI](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai); [F] — [MinerU README](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU)

### Inferences
- Worth it: a flagship or 8 GB-class phone, pages with tables/formulas/multi-column layout, output wanted as Markdown/HTML/LaTeX, tolerance for 20–60 s per page, and a review step for critical fields (silent rewriting risk).
- Not worth it: receipts, labels, signs, IDs and any plain-line text; anything needing word-level boxes and confidences; real-time or batch scanning; phones with ≤4 GB RAM or without a supported GPU. Here the classic pipeline wins on speed, memory, battery, boxes and — on vendor tests — raw text accuracy.
- A practical middle path on capable phones is classic OCR for everything plus an on-demand VLM call only for regions a layout model labels as table or formula (smaller crops also sidestep the 512×512 input limit).
- Handwriting quality of sub-1B models on device is unproven: the models with explicit handwriting claims are 5B-class or general VLMs.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of a sub-1B VLM against PP-OCRv5/v6 mobile on the same receipt or handwriting dataset, on device, was found.
- No independent evaluation of structured-JSON extraction accuracy for on-device small VLMs was found.
